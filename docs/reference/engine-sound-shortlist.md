# Engine catch and running sound: CC0 shortlist (ADR 0028)

Researched 2026-10-12. Read-only; no repository file changed, no audio downloaded.

## How this was checked

- **Searches.** Freesound with the `license:"Creative Commons 0"` filter, about 45 queries (generator start/idle/running/startup, portable/small/gas generator, pull start, 2-stroke/two-stroke idle, chainsaw/brushcutter/strimmer/weed eater/lawnmower start and idle, water pump engine, and others). A general web search found no CC0 recording of an ET950/TG950 itself; the hits were parts listings and paid libraries (Artlist, Fab).
- **Licence.** Every sound listed below was fetched on its own page (generic User-Agent `OpenAmResearch/1.0`). Each page's licence link points to `http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/` ("Creative Commons 0"). None of the pages was CC-BY, CC-BY-NC or Sampling+.
- **Duration, format and description** come from the sound page.
- **Cut points** come from Freesound's own waveform image (780 px across the whole file, so each pixel is 20 to 80 ms). I decoded the image into a level envelope per 0.1 to 1 s. Nobody has listened to these. Treat every cut as a starting point, ±0.1 s, and move it to the nearest zero crossing when trimming in Blender. "Level" below is the share of full scale in that waveform image.
- **Background noise, wind and voices** are known only from what the author wrote. They are not checked by ear.

**A point worth knowing about the "running" sound.** A small generator does not idle the way a chainsaw or a mower does. Its governor holds it near 3,000 rpm (50 Hz output, which is Nigeria's mains frequency) whenever it runs. A Husqvarna 445 chainsaw idles at about 2,700 to 2,900 rpm, and it is a two-stroke of similar size (45 cc against 63 cc). Its steady idle is therefore close in pitch and texture to an ET950 running. A four-stroke Honda generator is smoother and lower in tone. That is why two-stroke chainsaw idle ranks high below, even though Honda generators are the "right" kind of machine.

**Fit with ADR 0028.** No real recording catches in 0.5 to 1.5 s and then sits at a steady level. Every engine here surges for 1.5 to 3.5 s after the catch before it settles. There are two ways to handle that. (1) Let `start` run a bit longer (about 3 to 4 s, catch plus surge plus settle) and start the loop as `start` ends. (2) Cut `start` short (about 1.5 s, while it is still revving) and crossfade into the loop. Option 1 sounds more like a real start. The ADR's "about 0.5 to 1.5 seconds" would need loosening for it. Budget: at the pipeline's 40 kbps Opus, 4 s of catch plus a 3 s loop is about 35 KB, well inside the 221 KB still free.

## Recommended pairs

1. **ElSchorscho 871136 (catch 2.80–6.80) + 871135 (loop 1.00–4.00).** A two-stroke, the same machine and mic in both, close and clean. The nearest in character to an ET950.
2. **Skybonator 709272 alone (catch 0.90–4.40, loop 8.00–11.00).** A real portable generator, one file, a clear catch and settle. Four-stroke tone.
3. **kyles 406892 alone (catch 29.10–32.00, loop 40.00–43.00).** A small pump or generator from the same recordist as `cord` and `fail`.

All three need listening before anything is committed. Points to check by ear: whether 871136's surge sounds like a start or like a rev; whether 709272 has hiss once raised; and whether there is Gaza street noise under 406892.
