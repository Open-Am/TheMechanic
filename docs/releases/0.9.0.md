# 0.9.0

The hand follows your pointer, and removal is deliberate.

## Changed

- The mechanic's hand is your hand. With a mouse it rests on whatever the cursor points at, its arm running off the bottom of the screen to a body just out of view; on a phone it comes in under your finger. Pressing closes it on what is under it: a grip, or a fingertip pinch for small parts. It looks the same over every part, whether or not it can come off (plan 0010, ADRs 0020, 0007).
- Removal is two deliberate steps. Double tap a part (double click, or press G with the mouse on it) and, if it can come off now, the hand loosens it: the spark plug turns out of its thread, bolted parts crack loose, clips pop, the hose tugs off. Then drag the loose part: it comes out along its path, follows your hand, and dropping it on the tray removes it. Dropped anywhere else it goes back, still loose. If a part cannot come off yet, a double tap does nothing (plan 0010, ADR 0020).
- Every other drag turns the machine, with the hand holding the spot it grabbed and keeping it under your pointer. That includes dragging a part that is not loose, so a drag never says whether a part could come off and a stray drag never pulls one off. Before, a drag along a part's path took it off at once (plan 0010, ADR 0020).
- Putting a part back carries it in the hand from the tray instead of a floating chip.
- Loose parts are pressed home before the cord is pulled.
- The working screen's hint now reads "Double tap a part to loosen it, then drag it to the tray".
- The hand is no longer hollow: the cuff's inner lip hugs the wrist, the forearm's cut end is closed, and the arm now runs 1.2 m, forearm and upper arm, so it always leaves the screen. 3,980 triangles and 110.9 KB, within the hand's 4,000 and 400 KB.
- ADRs 0016, 0017 and 0019 are superseded by ADR 0020; ADR 0015's hand paragraph is replaced by it. Scoring, the action log and the data are unchanged: a removal is logged when the part lands in the tray.

## Added

- `npm run verify:phone` removes every part by double tap and a drag to the tray, and checks that the open hand looks the same over a removable and a blocked part, a double tap on a blocked part does nothing, a drag on a blocked part or on a part that is not loose turns the machine and removes nothing, turning from the floor holds on and lets go, G loosens the part under the mouse, and a loose part dropped away from the tray goes back.
- 232 tests: 12 new, 15 removed with the retired pull gesture, reach and base-plate hold (235 in 0.8.0).
- The hand file gains an optional `tip_point` (the open hand's fingertips), checked by `npm run assets`.

## Removed

- The plan 0009 hold under the base plate, and the plan 0008 reach from the side a part comes off: the hand is always at the pointer now. Their tests went with them.

## Known gaps

- Bolts, screws and the spanner and screwdriver are not in yet: loosening frees a part by its motion type alone. They come in the next plan, then sound (ADR 0018).
- On a phone the hand's fingertips sit right under the finger; the planned 10 mm lift above the finger was left out to keep the hand exactly at the touch. If the finger hides too much, it can come back.
- Over empty space a press closes the hand on nothing, at the machine's depth.
- The frame rate was measured only in headless Edge with software rendering: 60 fps at 4x CPU throttle.
