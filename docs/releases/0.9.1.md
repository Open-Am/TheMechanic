# 0.9.1

The hand stays out of the generator and holds a steady, natural attitude. A fix to plan 0010 (ADR 0020), after the owner tried 0.9.0: "The hand is weird. It's going into the generator. [...] also, it rotates weirdly."

## Fixed

- The hand went into the generator. Only its fingertips were placed on the surface under the pointer; the palm, the curled fingers and the thumb could sink into it, and a hand pinching a small part low down (the spark plug, plug cap, fuel tap) ended inside the tank or cylinder head above it. Now about 60 points spread over the hand, as its fingers are curled at that moment, are kept outside the surface it rests on, and about ten of them are checked against the whole machine along the camera's line of sight: if any is behind the machine's surface, the whole hand comes towards the camera. It stays under the pointer on screen and only its depth changes. The part the hand holds does not count, so fingers can curl behind what they grip.
- The hand rotated weirdly. It turned its palm to face whatever surface was under the pointer, so it rolled sharply at every edge, and while loosening it rode with the part's own rotation, spinning round with the spark plug. Now it keeps one steady attitude relative to the screen, palm down towards the scene, as a hand reaching into a scene looks, and when it holds a part it follows the part's position, never its spin.
- The arm passed very close to the camera and filled half the screen. It is now aimed at a point in front of the camera, low and to the right, outside the view: it still leaves through the bottom right of the screen but looks its real size.

## Added

- `npm run verify:phone` measures the share of the hand hidden behind the machine's surface while hovering over every part and the base, while loosening and while starting to carry, and fails above 10 percent. Measured at most 9 percent (a fingertip at an edge), against 71 percent before the fix.
- 236 tests, up from 232: the steady attitude and the push out of a surface.

## Known gaps

- The whole-machine check tests about ten points of the hand, every third frame while hovering and every frame while holding a part; a fingertip can still touch into an edge for a moment.
- The frame rate was measured only in headless Edge with software rendering: 60 fps at 4x CPU throttle while turning; the diagnostic overlay showed 28 to 60 fps while hovering, unthrottled, in the same software renderer. A real phone's GPU is faster at drawing but its CPU may be slower at the checks; this needs a phone.
