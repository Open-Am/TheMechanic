# 0.10.0

Bolts, screws and nuts, and the spanner and screwdriver in the hand.

## Added

- Bolts, screws and nuts on the parts that have them. When you loosen one of these parts, the hand takes a spanner or a screwdriver and turns each fastener out before the part comes free. The fasteners go with the part and are done up again when it goes back (plan 0011, ADR 0020).
- The fasteners, all drafts marked unverified: the fuel tank has 4 screws, the recoil starter 4 bolts, the muffler 2 nuts on the exhaust studs and 2 bolts through the heat shield, the carburettor 2 nuts on the flange studs, the alternator cover 4 bolts and the air filter door 2 bolts. They are in `machine.json` (the `fasteners` field, checked by `validate.ts`) and in the model, one node each under its part, named by its id (plan 0011, ADRs 0003, 0020).
- The sequence: the tool comes in from just off screen in 0.3 s. Each fastener takes 0.25 s: 2 turns, backing out 8 mm. The part comes free in 0.3 s and the tool goes back in 0.3 s, about 1.9 s for four bolts. On a put-back each fastener goes in again in 0.15 s. The screwdriver turns with the screw, and the spanner swings in 60 degree strokes. Details are in `docs/design/animation.md`.
- `tools.glb`: a 10 mm open-ended spanner and a Phillips screwdriver, 1,160 triangles and 48.6 KB, within the tools' 2,000 and 100 KB. They are made by `tools/blender/tools.py` and checked by `npm run assets`. If the file is missing, the fingers turn the fasteners. With no model, fasteners are drawn as small grey cylinders.
- `npm run verify:phone` checks the fasteners and the tools:
  - On the fault rounds, every fastened part that comes off has all its fasteners at least 6 mm out before it is loose, and has them done up after it goes back.
  - In a round of its own, it takes off the fuel tank (no fault needs it) and checks that the hand holds the screwdriver throughout. It also takes off the recoil starter and checks for the spanner. Both parts go to the tray and come back with their fasteners done up.
  - It pulls the cord while the starter's bolts are coming out, and checks that the spanner is put away and the bolts go back in.
- `tools/blender/match.py only=<id>` keeps the part's fasteners in the picture. There are new match images of six parts with their fasteners (local only, ADR 0013). `assets/gen950/STATUS.md` records each part's fasteners and what still differs.
- Dev-only hooks for the browser checks: `tool()`, `toolVisible()`, `fasteners(id)`, `handNeed()` and `setOrbit(azimuth, polar)`. `HIDDEN_DEBUG=1 npm run verify:phone` logs and screenshots a hand over the hidden limit.
- 262 tests, up from 246 in 0.9.4, covering the fastener data contract, the fastener pose and sequence, the model and tools checks, and the check for an unbaked model.

## Fixed

- Pulling the cord while a part's fasteners were coming out left the tool in the hand, floating at the machine, and the fasteners snapped back in only at the end. Now the tool is put away and the fasteners go back in as the part is pressed home. The new browser check failed before this fix and passes now.
- The shipped `model.glb` had none of the fasteners. It was older than the fastener modelling, so the game drew grey stand-in cylinders over the old drawn-in bolts. Rebuilding it showed that the `.blend` had been remodelled without a re-bake, which gave an untextured model, and the asset check passed it with 0 errors. The model has now been re-baked and re-exported: 23,658 triangles of 60,000 (fasteners 2,048 of their 3,000), 1.04 MB of 5 MB. The asset check now rejects any material without a base colour texture (test first).

## Checked

- `npm test` 262 passed; `npm run typecheck`, `npm run docs:check`, `npm run build` pass; `npm run size` 183.1 KB of 350 KB.
- `npm run assets`: machine 23,658 triangles, 1,038.6 KB, 20 fastener nodes, 0 errors; hand 3,980 triangles, 110.9 KB; tools 1,160 triangles, 48.6 KB.
- `npm run verify:phone` (exit 0): 6 of 6 faults; forced walk-out ok; fasteners undone and done up again on all six fastened parts; tools seen: spanner, screwdriver; the tool round passed (fuel tank with the screwdriver, recoil starter with the spanner, cord pulled mid-loosening); hand at most 5% hidden; 119 fps at 4x CPU throttle; 0 console errors. Two earlier runs of six on this branch failed with "hand: 12% of it went inside the machine, starting to carry capacitor": see Known gaps.
- `npm run verify:drop` (exit 0): 96 of 96 removals first time, machine never under the controls, frame rate held, 0 console errors.

## Known gaps

- None of the fasteners has been checked on a real machine. The match images suggest that the alternator cover's bolts go through its rim, not its face. They also suggest that the tank's screws go through side flanges the model does not have. The air filter door may be clipped rather than bolted. All three are in `docs/open-questions.md`.
- The tools come from just off screen at the lower left. The toolbox (ADR 0021, plan 0012) is not built.
- `HAND.maxPush` (the hand comes at most 0.2 m towards the camera to clear the machine) still lets the hand be hidden at some angles. This is known from 0.9.4 (the spark plug, 20% behind the tank) and waits on the owner. It has now also been seen when carrying the capacitor out of the alternator housing from above (azimuth -0.5, polar 0.35, 12% hidden). The phone check reaches that angle when the muffler round comes just before the capacitor round, so it fails in about one run in three. A new dev hook, `setOrbit`, reproduces it: it happens the same with the old model, and it clears with the cap raised to 0.6 m. The same sweep showed the hand going behind the machine while the spanner turns alternator cover bolts that face away from the camera (azimuth 1.0, polar 1.0). The phone check samples a loosening only once, 0.35 s in, so it does not see this.
- The frame rate was measured only in headless Edge with software rendering. The phone preview has not been republished with this release.
