# 0.9.3

Taking parts off works on the first try, all the way through a round. From the owner, 2026-10-11: "we're still having issues with dropping items. as soon as you have dropped like three or four of them, it becomes more difficult to drop. I think you should test this properly and make sure it's working well."

## What was wrong

A new browser check (below) took all twelve parts off, one after another, with mouse events and with touch events, dropping at four places, and counted the tries for each step. Before this release:

- **The drop itself was never the problem.** Every carry that started was removed by its first drop, at every drop place, with mouse and touch.
- **The tray grew over the machine.** The tray adds a row every two chips and pushes the pull cord up, but the machine was framed for the whole screen. On a 360 by 640 screen (a common Android phone with the browser's own bars) the drop strip reached the machine's feet after three parts, the pull cord covered them after five, and with eleven parts off the controls covered the lower 186 px of the machine. On 390 by 844 the pull cord reached the machine after seven parts. Presses there land on the disabled pull cord or on a chip, where a drag puts a part back instead, and a carried part goes out of sight under the chips on its way down. This matches "after three or four".
- **The double tap missed, most of all on touch.** It was timed from the first lift to the second lift, by the clock when the page got round to each event. That window includes the second press itself, and touch events reached the game 55 to 80 ms late. Touch double taps measured 290 to 390 ms against the 300 ms window: three touch rounds stopped at the first, second or third part because a part would not loosen, and the fourth was stopped by hand still on the first part. With a mouse, 4 of 44 missed. A missed double tap looks exactly like a part that cannot come off yet (ADR 0007), so it felt as if the game had stopped answering.
- **A thumb's second tap could land on a neighbour.** On a thin part such as the fuel hose, a second tap 3 px off showed the part behind, and did not count as the same part.

Not supported by the data: the frame rate falling as parts come off (it held steady), animations piling up (none left running), the carried part or the hand not resetting after a drop.

## Changed

- The machine is framed in the space above the pull cord and tray, and glides up as the tray grows a row; if that space gets too short for it, the camera stands back. It keeps at least 40 percent of the screen's height. The canvas still runs to the bottom of the screen, so the hand and a carried part are drawn over the controls as before.
- The double tap is timed from the first tap lifting to the second tap coming down, within 300 ms as before, using the events' own time stamps. The second press may last up to 500 ms. A tuning of ADR 0020's double-tap window, which the ADR leaves to the render layer; no rule changes.
- The second tap counts while the first tap's part is still under the finger by its finger-sized hit volume (`docs/design/interface.md`: every part has one at least 44 px across), even if a neighbour shows there. The same for every part, removable or not (ADR 0007).
- "Drop it here" is laid over the tray instead of added below it, so the controls no longer grow while a part is carried, and it brightens with the tray when the part is over the drop zone.

## Added

- `tools/verify/drop-play.ts` (`npm run verify:drop`): plays a round per input (mouse events, and touch events with touch emulation) and drop place (on the chips, just above the pull cord, at the very bottom, on the pull cord), taking all twelve parts off where a player would aim, and prints a table of tries per step, where the press landed, the controls' top edge against the machine's lowest point, the double tap's timing and input lag, and the frame rate from the diagnostic overlay. `--viewport` sets the screen size. Screenshots after 0, 4, 8, 11 and 12 parts. Exits 1 unless every step worked first time and the machine stayed clear of the controls. After this release: 96 of 96 removals first time at 390 by 844 (both inputs, all four drop places), and the 360 by 640 rounds listed in the commit.
- Both browser checks take `OPEN_AM_PORT` and `OPEN_AM_DEBUG_PORT`, so two can run at once, and end the browser's whole process tree on Windows (a plain kill left Edge's helper processes running).
- 244 tests, up from 237: the double-tap window, the second tap a few pixels off, and framing above the controls.

## Known gaps

- The capacitor never shows from any side at the default tilt, even with everything else off; it comes off by tapping where it is, through its hit volume. Added to `docs/open-questions.md`.
- Frame rates and input lag in the check are from headless Edge with software rendering on a PC, not a phone.
